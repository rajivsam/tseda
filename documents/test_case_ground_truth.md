# Benchmark Test-Case Ground Truth

## Purpose

This document defines the external and synthetic ground truth for the 11 datasets
used by `tests/test_comprehensive_dataset.py`. It supports Item 2 of the
publication roadmap: checking edge cases against what is known about each series
rather than treating the current `tseda` output as truth.

“Documented ground truth” below comes from a named public source or, for
`white_noise_data`, from the repository's generator. “Analyst classification” is
the expected testing interpretation used for `tseda`; it is not represented as a
claim made by the original data publisher. Research was refreshed on 25 November
2026.

## Ground-truth table

| Dataset | Documented source, measure, and coverage | Frequency / local subset | Documented characteristics | Analyst ground truth and test expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `coffee_prices` | FRED `PCOFFOTMUSDM`, **Global price of Coffee, Other Mild Arabica**, sourced from the International Monetary Fund. The unit is U.S. cents per pound, not seasonally adjusted, and values are nominal global-market benchmark prices. The local series has 426 monthly observations from 1990-01 through 2025-06. [FRED series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCOFFOTMUSDM) · [FRED data](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/PCOFFOTMUSDM) | Monthly; 426 rows locally | FRED documents monthly, nominal, non-seasonally-adjusted commodity prices. It does not assert a deterministic seasonal decomposition. | **Financial / regime edge case.** Expect stochastic trend, long price regimes, and volatility clustering or variance shifts; do not require fixed seasonality. A low seasonal score is plausible. The local file starts in 1990, while the current FRED metadata reports 1992 onward, so pre-1992 values require provenance verification. |
| `white_noise_data` | Synthetic i.i.d. Gaussian white noise generated by `WhiteNoiseDataLoader`: `np.random.normal(0, 1, 720)`, with 30 days of hourly timestamps. This is a repository-defined null, not an external observational dataset. [Local generator](../src/tseda/dataloader/white_noise_data_loader.py) | Hourly; 720 rows | By construction: zero mean, unit scale, independent observations, no trend, seasonality, or persistent component. | **Pure Noise.** The suitability gate should reject it, and no trend or seasonal reconstruction should be accepted. A chance change-point finding on repeated random samples is possible, but it should not create a structurally meaningful positive result. |
| `trimmed_biomass` | `generation biomass` from the Spain hourly energy dataset redistributed as **Hourly energy demand generation and weather**. The collector identifies ENTSO-E as the source of generation data. The quantity is Spanish grid biomass generation in MW. The local file contains a 2018-04-12 to 2018-07-04 subset. [Kaggle dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/nicholasjhana/energy-consumption-generation-prices-and-weather) | Hourly; 1,998 rows locally | The public source documents hourly 2015–2018 Spanish generation data and the exact `generation biomass` field. It does not publish a biomass-specific SSA decomposition. | **Trend-Dominated.** Expect operational level/regime changes and irregular dispatch variation, with no guaranteed stable seasonal cycle. A trend-dominated or regime-shift result is defensible; fixed seasonality should be treated cautiously. |
| `monthly_car_sales` | The widely redistributed benchmark **Monthly car sales in Quebec, 1960–1968**. The local series contains 108 monthly sales counts. It is documented through classic forecasting datasets and tutorials; a current primary Statistics Canada release could not be verified. [Benchmark copy](https://github.com/sspheng/Datasets/blob/main/Monthly-car-sales-in-quebec-1960.csv) · [Worked-series example](https://rstudio-pubs-static.s3.amazonaws.com/978117_80b9ce72644f412b8f51fe2197db8006.html) | Monthly; 108 rows, 1960-01 through 1968-12 | The canonical teaching interpretation is upward trend plus annual seasonality, often with changing amplitude. The exact primary publication is less transparent than for the official government series in this table. | **Trend + Seasonality / short-sample edge case.** Expect a 12-month seasonal structure and a positive trend, but require caution because only nine annual cycles are available. Item 1's residual-variance detector may be useful for changing amplitude, but short final segments must not be over-interpreted. |
| `uci_air_quality_hourly_co` | UCI **Air Quality**, dataset 360: hourly averages from a gas multisensor device and certified analyzer in an Italian city. `CO(GT)` is the true hourly CO concentration from the reference analyzer, in mg/m³. UCI documents March 2004–February 2005 and 9,357 instances; `-200` denotes missing data. `tseda` retains the last 2,000 valid rows locally. [UCI dataset page](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/360/air+quality) · [UCI API metadata](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/api/dataset?id=360) | Hourly; 2,000-row tail, 2005-01-07 through 2005-04-04 | UCI explicitly documents hourly reference-analyzer CO and the missing-value sentinel. It does not publish an official trend/seasonality decomposition. | **High-Noise Operational / Trend + Seasonality.** Expect strong diurnal and weekly effects from human activity plus weather and traffic noise. A daily 24-hour component is physically plausible; seasonality is analyst inference rather than an official UCI label. Missing rows dropped before analysis can alter spacing assumptions and must remain visible. |
| `ticket_resolution_hourly_nyc311` | NYC Open Data **311 Service Requests from 2010 to Present**, Socrata ID `erm2-nwe9`. Each source row is a service request. `tseda` derives hourly counts by grouping the request `closed_date`, regularizes missing hours to zero, and uses a rolling 30-day default window. [NYC dataset metadata](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/metadata/v1/erm2-nwe9/) · [Local loader](../src/tseda/dataloader/ticket_resolution_data_loader.py) | Hourly; 721 rows, currently 2026-04-04 through 2026-05-04 | NYC documents a daily-updated, case-level service-request feed. “Hourly resolved-ticket count” is a `tseda` transformation, not a native published metric. No official seasonal decomposition exists. | **High-Noise Operational.** Expect strong time-of-day and day-of-week patterns, many structural zeros, bursts, and variance changes. The large noise share and heteroscedasticity are plausible. Results are time-dependent because this is a live rolling snapshot, so a fixed benchmark value must not be assumed. |
| `hyndman_arrivals_quarterly_japan` | `fpp2::arrivals[, "Japan"]`: quarterly international arrivals to Australia from Japan, in thousands, 1981 Q1–2012 Q3. [fpp2 reference](https://rdrr.io/cran/fpp2/man/arrivals.html) | Quarterly; 127 rows locally, 1981-01 through 2012-07 | The package documents units and coverage. The textbook series is used to illustrate strong quarterly seasonality and differing long-run regimes. The interpretation of rise/decline stages is textbook commentary rather than metadata. | **Trend + Seasonality / short-regime edge case.** Expect annual quarterly seasonality and changing trend regimes. The final segment is short enough that DW just below the nominal 1.5 target should be reported as a boundary/cap behavior, not silently treated as exact model failure. |
| `hyndman_goog_daily_close` | `fpp2::goog`: split-adjusted NASDAQ closing prices for GOOG, 1,000 consecutive trading days; the fpp2 help describes a 25 February 2013–13 February 2017 span. [fpp2 reference](https://pkg.robjhyndman.com/fpp2/reference/goog.html) | Daily trading days; 1,000 rows locally, 2013-02-25 through 2017-02-10 | Hyndman uses the series as a financial random-walk example. Price levels have stochastic persistence; deterministic seasonality is not an expected property. The local endpoint is three calendar days earlier than the help-page endpoint despite the same stated row count, so the extraction should be version-checked. | **Financial / short-sample edge case.** Treat the level as near-random-walk/stochastically trending. Expected: little or no defensible deterministic seasonality; first differences are closer to white noise. A `Seasonality = ✓` result is a false-positive safeguard target, not ground truth. |
| `hyndman_hyndsight_daily_pageviews` | `fpp2::hyndsight`: daily page views of Rob Hyndman's Hyndsight blog for one year beginning 30 April 2014. Frequency is explicitly set to 7 to model the weekly pattern. [fpp2 reference](https://pkg.robjhyndman.com/fpp2/reference/hyndsight.html) | Daily; 365 rows, 2014-04-30 through 2015-04-29 | Weekly seasonality is explicitly documented by the package. | **Trend + Seasonality.** Expect a seven-day cycle and possibly level/campaign-driven changes. | `hyndman_sunspot_monthly_area` | The local values correspond to the monthly sunspot-area archive underlying annual `sunspotarea`: Royal Greenwich Observatory daily observations beginning May 1874, followed by USAF and NOAA compilations. [Annual fpp2 reference](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/fpp2/versions/2.5/topics/sunspotarea) · [Monthly archive](http://solarcyclescience.com/AR_Database/sunspot_area.txt) · [Archive description](https://zenodo.org/records/17108109) | Monthly; 1,709 rows, 1874-05 through 2016-09 | The approximately 11-year solar cycle is the established scientific pattern. Variation in cycle length/amplitude and historical calibration changes are part of the ground truth. The file is not the annual fpp2 object itself; this monthly provenance is inferred from the start date, units, and values and should be recorded with that qualification. |
| `hyndman_usconsumption_quarterly_consumption` | The local 1960–2016 quarterly percentage-change values match the `Consumption` column of `fpp2::uschange`, sourced via FRED. It is U.S. personal consumption expenditure percentage change, not a level. [fpp2 reference](https://pkg.robjhyndman.com/fpp2/reference/uschange.html) | Quarterly; 227 rows, 1960-01 through 2016-07 | The source documents percentage changes for the U.S. from 1960 to 2016. Percentage-change macroeconomic series are commonly treated as noisy and near-stationary, with recession shocks; that interpretation is textbook/domain inference. | **High-Noise Operational / financial-macro edge case.** Expect weak deterministic seasonality, substantial residual variation, and recession-related shocks. A trend or seasonal label should have low confidence. The file name is ambiguous: its coverage matches `fpp2::uschange`, not the older `fpp::usconsumption` (1970–2010). |

## Archetype assignments

| Archetype | Datasets | Ground-truth question for validation |
|---|---|---|
| Pure Noise | `white_noise_data` | Does the suitability gate reject the series without fabricating trend or seasonality? |
| Trend-Dominated | `trimmed_biomass` | Does the pipeline preserve regime/level changes without forcing a stable cycle? |
| Trend + Seasonality / Cyclicity | `monthly_car_sales`, `uci_air_quality_hourly_co`, `hyndman_arrivals_quarterly_japan`, `hyndman_hyndsight_daily_pageviews`, `hyndman_sunspot_monthly_area` | Are the expected periods found at the correct scale (12 months, 24 hours/weekly, 4 quarters, 7 days, approximately 11 years), without treating all cyclic behavior as fixed calendar seasonality? |
| High-Noise Operational | `uci_air_quality_hourly_co`, `ticket_resolution_hourly_nyc311`, `hyndman_usconsumption_quarterly_consumption` | Does the workflow expose residual uncertainty, structural zeros, and heteroscedasticity instead of over-explaining irregular variation? |
| Financial / Short-Sample Edge Cases | `coffee_prices`, `hyndman_goog_daily_close`, `monthly_car_sales`, `hyndman_arrivals_quarterly_japan`, `hyndman_usconsumption_quarterly_consumption` | Are stochastic persistence, short windows, regime changes, and boundary diagnostics handled separately from deterministic trend/seasonality? |

Datasets intentionally appear in more than one archetype where one describes the
data-generating context and another describes the relevant testing risk.

## Item 2 acceptance checks derived from the ground truth

1. **Random-walk safeguard:** `hyndman_goog_daily_close` must not be cited as
   deterministic seasonality without an explicit warning or first-difference
   verification. Its textbook ground truth is stochastic persistence.
2. **Short-sample DW framing:** for `hyndman_arrivals_quarterly_japan`, report
   `[1.5, 2.5]` as a diagnostic target range rather than a universal hard boundary.
   Document that recruitment/component caps and short quarterly history can stop
   optimization just below 1.5.
3. **Variance change points:** for `monthly_car_sales` and
   `ticket_resolution_hourly_nyc311`, compare any Item 1 variance marker with raw
   local dispersion and retain timestamps. For the NYC series, structural zeros
   and live-snapshot selection are alternative explanations for an apparent
   variance shift.
4. **Period correctness:** validate periodicity against the source scale, especially
   weekly Hyndsight page views and the approximately 11-year sunspot cycle.
5. **Provenance exceptions:** treat coffee data before 1992, the exact source of the
   monthly car-sales benchmark, the monthly sunspot extraction, and the
   `usconsumption`/`uschange` naming as disclosed provenance caveats—not resolved
   facts.

## Source-quality notes

- FRED/IMF, UCI, NYC Open Data, and the Hyndman R-package pages are first-party or
  canonical documentation for the corresponding local transformations.
- The biomass file is an ENTSO-E-derived Kaggle redistribution, so the local
  transformation and trim date must be retained in any reproducibility statement.
- The coffee and monthly-car-sales series are benchmark copies. Their current
  values should be refreshed only with an explicitly recorded source/version.
- The 311 feed is not a fixed benchmark. Its timestamp window and retrieval date
  must accompany any result.
- `tseda`'s own diagnostics are intentionally excluded as ground truth; the
  existing benchmark results are hypotheses to be compared with this table.
